# Trade note template

Synthetic: the example below is made up to show the layout. It describes no real trader, trade or account.

Use exactly this layout for every trade note, unless the project's own files give a different one. Write it in the trader's own first-person voice, in plain words.

There's **no overview section**: entry, exit, profit or loss and times already come from the journal's own trade statistics, so repeating them only adds noise.

## Layout

Journals usually keep headings, bold, lists, quotes and dividers, and drop tables and images, so the note uses only those.

```
<h3>✅ Done well</h3>
<ul>
<li><strong>{Short label}:</strong> {what was done well}</li>
</ul>
<hr>
<h3>🎯 Key takeaways</h3>
<blockquote>{one or two short sentences: the headline lesson}</blockquote>
<ul>
<li><strong>Lesson 1 · {Topic}:</strong> {key lesson}</li>
<li><strong>Lesson 2 · {Topic}:</strong> {improvement for future trades}</li>
<li><strong>Missed opportunity:</strong> {optional}</li>
<li><strong>Other factors:</strong> {optional: market conditions, tiredness, interruptions, mood}</li>
</ul>
<hr>
<p><strong>Setup details · {N} of 5</strong></p>
```

- **Done well:** two or three items. Always keep this section, even on a losing trade.
- **Key takeaways:** the quote first, then the lessons. Leave out the optional lines when there's nothing to say, rather than writing "none".
- **Setup details:** the total only. The ratings themselves go in the chat as a table, for the trader to paste into their own rating table.

## Writing numbers

- Prices are written without a currency sign, for example **71.42**.
- The currency sign is used only for amounts of money: risk, and profit or loss, for example $150.

## Example

```
<h3>✅ Done well</h3>
<ul>
<li><strong>Patience:</strong> I waited for the retest instead of chasing the first break.</li>
<li><strong>Stop on structure:</strong> my stop sat above the last swing high and I never moved it further away.</li>
</ul>
<hr>
<h3>🎯 Key takeaways</h3>
<blockquote>The setup was right; I gave back profit by holding past my own exit rule.</blockquote>
<ul>
<li><strong>Lesson 1 · Exits:</strong> when my exit rule triggers, I act on the close instead of hoping.</li>
<li><strong>Lesson 2 · Preparation:</strong> mark the target before entering, so I'm not deciding it mid-trade.</li>
<li><strong>Other factors:</strong> tired after a long session; mood went from calm to uneasy after the stop move.</li>
</ul>
<hr>
<p><strong>Setup details · 3.5 of 5</strong></p>
```
